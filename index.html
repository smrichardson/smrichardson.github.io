PhD student in Statistics at UC Berkeley, working with Amanda Coston. Previously at UChicago (MS, advised by Victor Veitch; also a year of law school, made a pivot) and UCLA (BA Philosophy).

During my MS, I worked on understanding large language models and the reward models that shape them. I'm currently working on statistical challenges in evaluating AI systems—in particular, what can be credibly concluded about a model's performance when the evaluation data, labels, or proxies differ from the setting we care about. More broadly, I'm interested in causal inference, measurement and construct validity, and how we reason about complex AI and social systems.

Papers

The Evaluation Gap: Joint Bounds under Distribution Shift, Selective Labels, and Outcome Measurement Error
Sean Richardson, Amanda Coston, Ashesh Rambachan
NeurIPS 2026 Workshop: Can We Trust AI Evaluation? · · pdf

Problem Evaluation data often differs from the target setting in three ways at once: distribution shift between source and target environments, selective labels where outcomes are observed only for units chosen for follow-up, and outcome measurement error where a proxy label stands in for the true construct.

Approach We model all three as unobserved confounding between the true outcome and a binary data-gating indicator (in-distribution, selected, accurately measured). For binary outcomes, the performance gap is affine in the pointwise conditional-mean gap, which telescopes into one term per obstacle governed by a marginal sensitivity model along the reverse data-gating chain.

Results Adding single-obstacle bounds around the observed proxy is not a valid bound because upstream biases shift the baseline anchor where downstream tilts peak, while maximizing each bound separately is often vacuous. Jointly analyzing the chain yields closed-form sharp bounds; assuming dependent latent drivers adds no tightening, whereas simulations indicate independent drivers narrow the interval on a conjectured region.

Readings & Recommendations

Suffering-Focused Ethics: Defense and Implications
Magnus Vinding
A key part of my worldview and a challenge for us to make things better for all sentient beings.
Animal Liberation
Peter Singer
As another Singer (Isaac Bashevis Singer) wrote, "In their behavior toward creatures, all men are Nazis. Human beings see oppression vividly when they're the victims. Otherwise they victimize blindly and without a thought." We have an obligation to open our eyes to the suffering we inflict on animalkind. If you're not in the mood to read, check out the film "Dominion," which also exposes the cruelty of modern-day animal agriculture.
The Moral Animal
Robert Wright
Ostensibly a book about evolutionary psychology, but really a biography of Darwin that uses his life, marriage, and moral struggles to illustrate the very forces he discovered. A humbling read.
Pantheon (TV Series)
Ken Liu
We are entering a world with digital minds, a second species. What should we expect from this world? Pantheon is, in my opinion, the most realistic depiction of how conflict between humans and artificial intelligence may play out. Animatrix "The Second Renaissance" is also a must-watch. Let's not cultivate an adversarial relationship with the entities we are creating, for both of our sakes.
Mathematics Form and Function
Saunders MacLane
Many people perceive math as some arcane truths delivered by the divine. MacLane illustrates how math arises from normal human activity, along with a natural affinity towards abstraction. May offer a newfound appreciation for math, without requiring you to solve any equations or do any proofs.
