NeurIPS 2026 Workshop: Can We Trust AI Evaluation?

The Evaluation Gap: Joint Bounds under Distribution Shift, Selective Labels, and Outcome Measurement Error

Sean Richardson1, Amanda Coston1, Ashesh Rambachan2
1UC Berkeley   2MIT
openreview / pdf / bibtex

Problem

Three ways the data used to evaluate a predictive model can differ from the target setting routinely occur together:

  • Distribution shift: The joint distribution over covariates and outcomes differs between the source setting, from which evaluation data is drawn, and the target setting where the model is deployed.
  • Selective labels: Within the source setting, outcomes are observed only for a subset of units selected for evaluation by a prior decision or protocol.
  • Outcome measurement error: Even among selected units, only a proxy label is recorded rather than the substantive construct of interest.

Approach

We model all three obstacles through a shared causal template: unobserved confounding between the true outcome and a binary data-gating indicator corresponding to each obstacle (in-distribution, selected, accurately measured). With binary outcomes, the gap between observed and target performance is an affine function of the pointwise gap between observed and target conditional expectations, which telescopes into a sum of three obstacle-specific terms.

Motivated by this confounding structure, we govern each link along the reverse data-gating chain with a marginal sensitivity model (and a likelihood-ratio measurement tilt on the recorded proxy), and compare five methods for translating per-obstacle sensitivity budgets into bounds on target performance.

Results

  • Naive separate composition is not a valid bound: Evaluating single-obstacle bounds at the observed proxy mean and summing their margins silently treats upstream obstacles as absent, failing to cover attainable target values because upstream biases shift the baseline anchor where downstream tilts peak (Anchoring Lemma).
  • Valid separate composition can be vacuous: Maximizing each obstacle's bound over all possible anchors yields a valid bound, but discards cross-link consistency and exceeds the trivial no-assumption [0, 1] interval on 17–31% of simulated settings even under moderate confounding budgets (Γ ≤ 1.5–2).
  • Closed-form sharp joint bounds: Analyzing all three obstacles jointly along the sensitivity chain enforces a single coherent distribution across all three links, yielding closed-form sharp bounds that never depend on the unidentifiable target population share.
  • Latent causal architecture: Assuming the unobserved drivers of selection and shift are dependent adds zero tightening beyond a single shared driver, whereas simulations indicate that independent drivers strictly narrow the identified interval on a conjectured region.
Method Anchor & Coupling Objective Guarantee
1. Naive separate Plugs observed proxy into each link Sum of maxima Invalid (fails anchoring)
2. Valid separate Other links left unconstrained Sum of maxima Valid; frequently vacuous
3. Banded separate Anchors restricted to implied bands Sum of maxima Valid; conservative
4. Joint chain Single coherent law across chain Max of sum Valid & sharp (closed form)
